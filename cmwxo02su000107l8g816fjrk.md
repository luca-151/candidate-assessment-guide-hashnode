## Why this comparison exists

If an employer sends you a HireVue game-based assessment, you typically get a few days between the invitation and the deadline. The games themselves, memory, numeracy, pattern and emotion-recognition tasks played against the clock, are unfamiliar to most candidates the first time they see them, and the format, not the underlying ability, is what practice can fix.

Five services sell HireVue game practice. They differ a lot in how many of HireVue's games they cover, what you can try for free, and how they charge. This article compares them on one published rubric, using each vendor's own pages wherever possible.

## How we scored

Every tool is assessed on the same six criteria, the rubric published on our About page:

1. **Formats covered** how many of HireVue's game types the practice set includes.
2. **Free tier** what you can actually play before paying, not just read.
3. **Price and billing model** one-time pass, subscription, or lifetime, and what happens when it ends.
4. **Timing realism** whether practice runs against the clock, the way the real assessment does.
5. **Feedback quality** what you learn after a run: a score, a history, guidance on what to fix.
6. **Employer and platform coverage** whether the tool also helps if your next application uses a different assessment provider.

![The scoring rubric: six criteria applied to every tool](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/luca-151/candidate-assessment-guide-hashnode/main/images/article-01-rubric.svg align="center")

A note on what none of these tools can do: none is affiliated with HireVue, none has the actual test questions, and there is no "answer key" for game-based assessments, the games measure how you play, not what you memorise. Any service that promises you a score or a pass is promising something it cannot deliver.

## The tools, one by one

### JobTestPrep: the established course, the narrowest game set

JobTestPrep's HireVue Games Prep is a structured course: interactive guides and simulations for each game it covers, with score reports. Its product page lists the course at **$79** (checked 26 November 2026). JobTestPrep's own pricing FAQ sells three access windows, Short (3–7 days), Medium (7–30 days) and Long (1–3 months), with $79 as the short-plan example and $99 as the long-plan example (upgrading short to long costs the $20 difference). Note: a competitor comparison describes the tiers as Basic $79 / Advanced $89 / Premium $99, each for one month; that does not match the vendor's own FAQ, which prices by duration, so we use the vendor's version here.

The limitation is coverage. The course covers **6 of HireVue's game types** Numerosity, Flashback, Shapedance, Digitspan, Singularity and PortraitXT (per GameTestPrep's comparison, July 2026). If your invitation involves games outside that set, the course cannot rehearse them. There are no playable free games to try before paying: none is offered on the product page (checked 26 November 2026), and a competitor comparison likewise states there is no free tier.

**Best for:** candidates who know their assessment uses only the covered games and who prefer a guided course format over self-directed practice.

### GameTestPrep: the only real free tier, and the cheapest short pass

GameTestPrep covers **10 HireVue game types**, and it is the only tool in this comparison that lets you play before paying: Numerosity, Digitspan and Shapedance are **free forever, with unlimited attempts and no card required** (vendor's homepage, checked 26 November 2026).

Pro is sold as one-time passes, **$9 for 7 days, $19 for 30 days, $34 for 90 days** with nothing to cancel and nothing that renews; a further pass stacks on top of any time remaining. Pro unlocks every game, score history, timed mock assessments and full reports. The same account covers 78 games across 11 assessment providers, which matters if you are applying to several employers using different vendors.

**Best for:** most candidates on a deadline. Try the three free games first; if the format suits you, a 7-day pass at $9 is the lowest-cost way to rehearse the full set.

### Game Assessment Prep: the widest HireVue set, charged per category

Game Assessment Prep lists **13 HireVue-style simulations** Numerosity, Flashback, Pathfinder, Digitspan, Disconumbers, Puzzle Picture, Portrait, PortraitXT, Shapedance, E-Motions, Singularity, Teamchat and Pulse, the broadest HireVue-specific coverage in this comparison (vendor's site, checked 26 November 2026).

The pricing needs reading carefully. Articles, guides and your results are free, and you can sign in to check your trial access, but replays require a **category pass**: $19 for 7 days, $29 for 30 days or $39 for 90 days **per category** (cognitive, spatial, personality, emotions), with no automatic renewal. Rehearsing all 13 games repeatedly can therefore mean more than one pass. Note for readers comparing older reviews: several comparison pages published earlier in 2026 describe this service as a $40/month subscription covering 11 games; its own site now describes 13 games and per-category passes, and we have used the vendor's current pages.

The vendor reports 25,700+ registered candidates and 195,000+ completed practice sessions as of 24 September 2026 (vendor-reported figures, not independently audited).

**Best for:** candidates who want the widest HireVue-specific game set and are comfortable managing per-category passes.

### Graduates First: lifetime access, but not HireVue's games

Graduates First sells a pack of **8 game-based assessments** Balloon BART, Cognition-A, Cognition-M, i-EQ, MTA-Tray, MathBubbles, Flanker Task and PassCode, for a **one-time payment with lifetime access and unlimited practice** (vendor's product page, checked 26 November 2026). Two independent competitor comparisons agree the pack costs **£26.95 or €29.95 depending on region**, discounted from £49.95 / €59.95 (GameTestPrep, July 2026; Game Assessment Prep, January 2026, still showing €29.95 when re-checked 26 November 2026). We could not confirm that figure on the vendor's own page, because Graduates First's product page lists no price at all (re-checked live 26 November 2026) and shows it only at checkout, so treat the £26.95 / €29.95 figure as reported, not vendor-confirmed. Free numerical, logical and verbal reasoning tests are available to try before buying, but the games themselves are not free.

The important caveat: these are **not HireVue's games**. Built by psychologists in the style of SHL and similar providers, they train overlapping skills, risk, memory, attention, emotional recognition, but they will not show you the specific formats a HireVue invitation contains. Graduates First also sells a much larger aptitude-test catalogue, and reports being used by over 150 universities (vendor's site).

**Best for:** candidates facing SHL-style or mixed game-based assessments across several employers, who prefer paying once and keeping access. It is a supplement for a HireVue assessment, not a substitute for HireVue-specific practice.

### Candidate Falcon: full HireVue set inside a subscription, games plus interviews

As with every tool in this comparison, check our figures against the vendor's pricing page, as we did on 26 November 2026.

Candidate Falcon's games plans cover **11 HireVue games** the full set it lists, alongside practice for Arctic Shores (15 tasks), Cappfinity, Harver, Plum and the UK Civil Service tests. Pricing is a **monthly subscription**: **€12.99/month** for one game platform (you choose which, and it stays fixed for the subscription) or **€17.99/month** for all game platforms, cancellable anytime. There are free guides, but the games themselves sit behind the subscription, unlike GameTestPrep, there is no free game to play first (no trial is mentioned anywhere on the vendor's pricing page or FAQ, checked 26 November 2026).

Two honest limitations. First, a subscription is the wrong shape for a single assessment with a deadline this week: a one-time pass elsewhere costs less if you only need a few days. Second, the AI feedback Candidate Falcon advertises applies to its one-way video interview practice, which is a separate plan (€19.99/month and up); the games plans do not include it, per the vendor's own pricing table.

Where the subscription earns its price is breadth over time: games across six platforms plus company-specific interview questions, and video-interview practice if you take a combined plan (from €36.99/month). The vendor reports 6,000+ candidates trained ([candidatefalcon.com](http://candidatefalcon.com), checked 26 November 2026).

**Best for:** candidates applying to many employers over several months, especially where a HireVue assessment is followed by a one-way video interview.

## Which one should you pick?

- **Your assessment is in the next few days and it is definitely HireVue:** start with GameTestPrep's three free games today. If you need the rest, the $9 seven-day pass is the cheapest full rehearsal; Game Assessment Prep covers two to three more game types if an unusual game is your worry, priced per category.
- **You were told exactly which games you face, and they are the common ones:** JobTestPrep's course is a reasonable guided option at $79, but check its six-game list against your invitation first, the other tools cover more games for less money.
- **You are applying widely, over months, to employers using different providers:** Candidate Falcon's all-platforms subscription (€17.99/month) or GameTestPrep's 90-day pass ($34) both spread the cost; the subscription adds video-interview practice, the pass is cheaper and never renews.
- **Your assessments are SHL-style rather than HireVue:** Graduates First's lifetime pack is built for that, and paying once suits a long job search.
- **You pay nothing:** HireVue's own candidate guidance, Candidate Falcon's and Game Assessment Prep's free guides, and GameTestPrep's three free games are the honest free options. No free tool in this market covers the full HireVue set.

## What practice can and cannot do

Practice removes the format shock: you learn the rules, the clock and the controls before they count. It does not change the traits the games sample, it does not transfer between very different game types, and no practice tool can promise you a higher employer score or a job offer. Treat any such promise, from any vendor, as a reason to close the tab.
